“Today, a change in panel geometry or inspection area can mean repeated robot programming and physical trial runs. Our goal is to reduce that effort through a common motion planning framework, developed in collaboration with IRT Jules Verne.
Starting on the left, we automatically generate scan paths from measured surface information, including sensor point clouds. This means we can prepare inspection paths even when a complete CAD model is unavailable. Tesseract then generates the robot motion needed to follow those paths, reducing manual waypoint programming.
For detailed follow-up inspection, GTSP helps sequence visits to more than 100 defect locations efficiently. We rescan only the flagged areas. This reduces unnecessary robot travel and repeat scanning.
In the middle, we integrate with the NVIDIA Isaac Sim digital twin environment to check reachability, collision risks and scan coverage before running the physical robot. The results feed back into planning so we can refine the trajectory before approved execution on FANUC. The expected benefit is fewer physical trial runs and less time spent resolving motion issues in the inspection cell.
On the right is the broader value of the common platform. We are developing reusable components that support different planning options. When the part, robot or task changes, we can retain the shared software and adapt the planner, robot model and execution interface.
We have also completed benchmarking between the HPP and IRT approaches. That comparison helps guide planner selection against the requirements of each inspection use case. Future options include reinforcement learning and vision-language-action techniques.
The framework is still in development. The value we aim to demonstrate is less programming effort, more focused inspection and less repeated engineering across parts and robots
